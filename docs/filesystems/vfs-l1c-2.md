# VFS-L1C-2 publication arbitration

**Verdict: PARTIAL.** The reusable publication boundary is implemented and exercised independently of the two ordinary spawn paths. This phase does not qualify either ordinary adapter or the primary platform.

The registration runtime keeps the exact authenticated result, source receipt and grants, parent and child identities, deadline, and retain request until the publication decision. Transactional wait does not consume a result while its waiter is still armed. A receipt is provisional. Under scheduler → capability broker → registration runtime serialization, publication checks the exact private creation ticket, process incarnation and VM generation, live parent image and credentials, source binding and receipt, cwd, exact held child receipt, deadline, and the active trusted service image and endpoint owner. It reserves process and ready queue capacity before installing the binding. The binding is installed through the normal namespace table and tracked by the existing registration lease row.

Cancellation linearizes when the scheduler takes the private payload into its reclamation state. Publication transfers exclusive ownership out of the pending table and linearizes at scheduler insertion, after installing the authenticated binding and before enqueue. The scheduler guard spans removal of the private PID claim, insertion, and the single enqueue. Once the binding is installed there is no intervening fallible return or cancellation boundary. Registration retirement serializes with the transaction; if it follows publication it withdraws the binding through normal runtime retirement, leaving the committed process in scheduler ownership.

A losing cancellation cannot retrieve a published payload. Before publication, the RAII owner can cancel, hand off the exact remote claim, and reclaim VM and canonical stdio outside the scheduler guard. A delivered request, late acknowledgement or release obligation stays with registration ownership; the private slot can retain just the PID claim until its exact lease and caller delivery rows settle. Unsent cancellation can discard the queued request. Timeout and endpoint withdrawal do not prove settlement, and no additional KLC completion consumer was added. An unresponsive service can keep a claim charged indefinitely.

The host harness uses the actual pending state, retained receipt checks, binding installation and registration lifecycle, with process insertion and enqueue modeled. It passed all 103 prior tests plus 112 mixed arbitration cycles. The mixed test covers successful transfer, duplicate decisions, cancellation after acknowledgement, retirement before and after publication, parent exit, deadline rejection and failed preparation. Existing tests supply the bounded capacity, stale/late reply, correlated refusal and delayed cleanup cases. Pending state tests synchronize both ownership orders. See the JSON for paths and individual gates.

The isolated QEMU runs use 2 GiB and the existing copied VFS channel. The observer runs while init is parked and the fixture checks for absence from the ordinary process table and asserts on any scheduler selection before enqueue. The success case runs the existing `stat /tmp` utility with VFS metadata routing and records completion of the authenticated metadata response before clean exit and scheduler reaping. The cancellation cases exercise an unsent retain and a retained receipt before publication, then check that a second cancel and publication fail. The final and earlier traces are indexed separately in the JSON; an earlier SMP fixture run exposed a retry that could consume a terminal reply before its wake was processed.

ZRAM remains in ring 0. No swap or mmap path changed. The VFS-L1P fixture checks restored allocator and descriptor counts and no added swap candidates; successful publication also logs the exact child reaper counts. Those are distinct observations. The concurrent fixture does not prove whole-system heap, frame and descriptor counts return to a common baseline and does not qualify fault-driven swap-in.

The final isolated matrix passed on 1, 2 and 4 vCPUs. The unmodified ordinary Phase 3.0 gate at 2 GiB and 2 vCPUs failed: it reached Foundation Complete but missed the USB mouse and several VFS stat markers. That attempt does not establish causality. The guard still reports the same four primary findings and zero candidate findings; source equivalence found no unexpected drift across 12,346 baseline paths.

VFS-L1C-3 still needs each adapter to retain the non-cloneable local owner and dispose of it on parent exit, exec and failure. This boundary fences publication when the source or caller is invalid; it does not independently reclaim an abandoned adapter-owned handle. Session and account admission, general descriptor inheritance, and adapter-specific supervision still need integration. Native service retirement before publication, late acknowledgement after cancellation, and replacement have no qualification witness here. Native replacement remains `NOT_RUN`; no replacement provenance is added. Namespace construction still uses the established infallible allocator paths. These are limits on readiness for activation.
