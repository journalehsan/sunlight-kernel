# STACK-2B: Session readiness and native startup

**PARTIAL.** Fresh eager and demand boots both passed the existing session readiness gate. The historical demand timeout did not recur, so its cause is unresolved. The packaged fixture still failed its ordinary visibility check in two subsequent demand attempts.

The runs reused the STACK-2A eager and demand ISOs in fresh diskless 1 GiB BIOS guests with two vCPUs and QEMU 11.1.3 TCG. The resource archive extracted independently from both ISOs matched the preserved archive SHA-256 `8d24ea00884396e3ec3598539f89379310de4d7d4c42038cb8a63227b4db6a1a`. The 9,304-byte fixture was unchanged. The archived diagnostic runner retained its original readiness markers and 180-second timeout per marker.

In the historical timeout, sessiond was published as PID 32 and reported service readiness; the VFS inventory, shared 2 KiB read and namespace gates had already passed. The first confirmed functional divergence from historical eager was within the catalog scan: eager published routed stat calls for sessiond and discovered Welcome, while the failing demand log installed its routing policy but showed no routed stat publication from PID 32 and no eligible-bundles witness. The scan enumerates `/Applications`, reads and checks candidate manifests and stats the eligible executables. The logs do not identify the failing step or its return code. Other services, TTY ticks and the document recovery workload continued; the demand recovery sample had 166,238 free frames, a low water of 153,424 and no swap activity. This does not establish a scheduler stall or memory exhaustion.

Fresh matched runs reached both session checkpoints, discovered Welcome, passed the document workload and reached ordinary TTY input. At document recovery in the short matched pair, 32 leaders held 16,384 eager versus 478 demand stack frames. Free frames were 150,334 versus 166,238; minimum free frames were 137,520 versus 153,424. Both checkpoints recorded zero stored swap blocks. These are guest census figures, separate from fixture commitment.

In the short matched pair, eager stat returned a root-owned regular file of mode 0755 and size 9,304. Demand stat exited 1 after the utility started and its boot root routing policy was installed; no routed stat publication from that utility or subsequent fixture load was recorded. The isolated demand retry passed readiness and repeated the stat failure. The initial pair, which ran the longer TLS command sequence before the fixture check, also passed readiness but stat exited 1 in both configurations. Thus the short pair first diverges at stat, but packaged stat failure is not exclusive to demand. The diagnostic does not establish why publication was absent or connect that symptom causally to the historical catalog failure. Demand fixture ABI, exit, commitment and reclamation remain BLOCKED.

The short eager control ran `/bin/stackprobe b` via the normal foreground shell. It produced the Ring-3 witness after checking CPL, 16-byte alignment, argc, basic argv contents and termination, and walking envp/auxv to check the placement and nonzero contents of AT_RANDOM. The loader recorded `argc=2 envc=15 auxv=2 rsp=0x7fffffffedf0`. PID 42, incarnation 83, VM 93 produced one authoritative native terminal result, code 0, also returned to the shell. Retirement reclaimed 515 user frames, eight page-table frames and zero swap blocks, with zero IPC entries cleared. The three ELF leaf pages leave 512 stack frames (2 MiB), agreeing with the STACK-2A eager control. The scheduler reap marker follows process cleanup; independent inventory of all resource classes was not run.

No implementation change was made without a confirmed root cause. Eager remains the default; the opt-in demand stack, 2 MiB limit, adaptive heaps, PMM accounting, 16 MiB kernel heap, readiness contracts and ZRAM policy are unchanged. STACK-2A's downloaded `/tmp/stackprobe` visibility failure remains a separate unresolved VFS issue. Broader VFS migration, deep growth, Helios and worker stacks, and sustained 512 MiB operation were not tested.

Raw evidence is under `target/stack-2b`: `{eager,demand}-repeat/serial.log` and `demand-third/serial.log` with sibling runner logs; the initial pair is in `{eager,demand}/`. Historical evidence remains under `target/stack-2a`.
